Experimental studies on tempering hardened steel 
demonstrate that increasing the tempering temperature 
leads to a decrease in hardness and an increase in 
toughness. Tempering also reduces brittleness and 
improves ductility.

4. 
The Jominy end quench test is a standard method for 
determining the hardenability of steel, which is the ability 
of steel to transform from austenite to martensite upon 
cooling from a high temperature. The test involves heating 
a cylindrical steel sample, then quenching one end with 
water to create a cooling gradient. Hardness 
measurements are then taken along the sample's length to 
determine the depth of hardening.
